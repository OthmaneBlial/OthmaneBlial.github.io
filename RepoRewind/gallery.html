Exhibit 01 · 21 commits · complete main

The release became a workflow.

The first public landmark arrived early. Most of the history that followed was not another initial build—it was the work of turning an effect into something people could repeatedly use and trust.

The finding

v0.1.0 sits at commit 7 of 21. README.md then became the most frequently touched path at 16 commits. Average line churn fell from 1,607 per commit in the earlier 80% to 777 in the final 20%—a 52% reduction consistent with productization and consolidation after the foundation.

Do not over-read it

Churn counts added plus deleted lines and can be dominated by generated or reformatted files. It does not measure code quality, time, or effort.

Exhibit 02 · 79 commits · complete main

The monolith became packages.

Ruins are useful when they point to what replaced them. Here, deletion evidence and explicit refactor commits turn a missing file into an architectural story.

The finding

Root main.py accumulated 20 touches and 7,552 changed lines, then became dormant in the final quarter. At commits 41 and 42, delegation and command modules were explicitly extracted into packages; their former single-file paths remain deleted while the package directories stand.

Do not over-read it

A deleted path plus a similarly named package is strong structural evidence. RepoRewind never reads source contents, so it cannot claim that behavior remained equivalent.

Exhibit 03 · 183 commits · complete main

A rename rebuilt the city.

A project rename is a visible rupture, but the release evidence around it shows why the migration is a midpoint—not a finale.

The finding

At commit 111, the repository recorded 32 renames across 54 changed files. The later v2.1.0 → desktop-preview-3.0.0 range spans 562 file changes and +26,699 net lines, making the rename the strongest migration event inside a larger expansion.

Do not over-read it

Rename evidence depends on Git's similarity detection. Net lines and file-change counts describe repository structure, not user impact or engineering value.
